Charter, on one map

Charter 0.4.8: who holds what, where work happens, the three lines a change crosses, and what the machines remember. It goes with the desk sheet (docs/cheatsheet.html), which covers what to do and when.

Lens

Hover or tap anything on the map to read what it is. Click to pin it, press Esc to unpin. The lens buttons light up one role at a time.

How to read the map

Regions, left to right, then the bottom

1 The owner's keys: the files that define the gates, and the owner's commands.
2 The written charter: scope, road, decisions, change requests.
3 The work: the unit of work and its five-step loop.
4 The gates: one engine run at three lines. Each column is darker than the one before because it is harder to get round.
5 main: what counts as accepted, and where the guarantees stop.
6 What the machines remember: the gate log, the cost ledger, the synthesis, and the evidence classes.
7 Where charter comes from: this repo, the installer, the tracks.

Who and what

Filled disc: the owner, the one person who rules and commits the keys.
Outlined disc: the agent. Claude Code, Codex or pi; one session writes one unit.
Folded page: a file in the repository. A dashed outline means the file is generated, never edited by hand.
Filled lock: the whole file is the owner's. Outlined lock: only some fields are (status, approval, an active decision's check).
Double-bordered stamp: an owner command that writes a ruling and commits it.
Pill: a step in the agent's loop. The green one is the step a gate judges.

What a gate does, at each line

Solid shield: refuses. The refusal names the way forward.
Half shield: refuses on the client track or with an opt-in, and warns otherwise. The detail panel says which.
Dashed diamond: warns and logs. Warnings that keep getting ignored are the candidates for becoming gates.
Dot: not checked at this line.
Green band: the path a change takes to main (commit, push, merge).

Links

Thin navy: the unit cites a record (scope:, rail:, charter:<id>).
Dashed grey: the agent proposes a record, and it waits on the owner.
Thick teal: the owner rules. Status, a ## Ruling line and a commit.
Dotted violet: written down by a machine, not by a person.
Grey: the package flowing from this repository into projects.

Reading it as a story

  1. The owner writes what is sold (scope.md) or the road (rails.md) and holds the files that define the gates (region 1).
  2. The agent starts a unit that cites a scope item or a rail and the decisions it relies on. It edits inside the unit's perimeter, runs the probes, pastes the evidence and closes the unit (region 3).
  3. A change passes through the same engine three times: as the agent edits, at commit, and in CI from a clean clone (region 4). Each line catches what the one before it can miss.
  4. What the agent cannot settle, it proposes: a decision or a change request. The owner rules on it with one command, which writes the ruling and commits it (the teal arrow).
  5. The machines keep the gate log, the cost of each session and an anchored progress synthesis (region 6). Nothing there is written by hand.
