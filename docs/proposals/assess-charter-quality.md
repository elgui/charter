# How much better prepared is this Charter?

Design proposal for the iteration after 0.4.27, 18 November 2026.

Give the owner a score for the quality of the current plan and an answer to “what could we improve next?” Start with the outcomes the project has promised in a named delivery horizon. For each, follow the intent through its specification, its position on the road, its rail and its executable units. Grade what a successor can establish from that chain. A release can have an excellent plan and still be waiting for a customer. Show the wait beside the score.

The first deliverable is a paired worksheet on a real outcome. The second is a read-only projection of that worksheet in Project Road. A third, conditional on the pilot, is delegation for a narrow class of repeated preparation work. These stages can be evaluated separately. Charter already distinguishes recording, checking, delivering and observing an outcome. Its delegate already reads project-specific authority. ## Twelve questions a second assessor can answer

Each row below describes the evidence for **2 points**. Award **1** for a supported part of that claim and name the missing part. Award **0** for a demonstrated contradiction, a counterexample, or an essential provision confirmed absent after inspecting its authoritative home. Enter **?** when the source is unavailable, stale, uninspected or disputed. Record the observation that would settle it. A claim in an older release can earn credit for that release. It needs fresh evidence to describe the current one.

| ID | Question for the assessor | What earns full credit |
|---|---|---|
| S1 | Who needs what behavior? | From a ruled source or explicitly identified draft, recover the actor, trigger, observable result and scope boundary. A second reader can tell which adjacent request falls outside it. |
| S2 | Where could two implementers reasonably disagree? | Trace a material constraint or assumption to its source. For a failure, permission boundary or unusual input, the spec gives one expected behavior or names the unresolved choice, its decider and what must wait. |
| S3 | Could the customer’s intent be checked? | A representative success and a plausible wrong result have distinguishable expected observations, including meaningful thresholds where relevant. Someone independent can apply them to the same examples and agree on both. |
| D1 | Why does this outcome come at this point on the road? | Its order follows a commitment, risk or enabling dependency. Identify the named consumer or decision it unlocks and what can wait. Test the rationale by asking what breaks if two items swap. |
| D2 | Does this path work across dependencies? | Trace required predecessors to authoritative records and evidence. Check cycles, missing links and shared prerequisites. Removing a critical prerequisite from a copy changes the proposed path or raises a hold. |
| D3 | Can a new worker take the right next step today? | On a pinned snapshot a cold reader recovers a feasible next action, actor, needed access and condition to resume. An explicit evidenced wait is a correct answer. A runner responsible for dispatch must agree or expose the discrepancy. |
| R1 | What useful change does this rail own? | Trace an observable milestone and its beneficiary to an adopted commitment, or identify the proposal and its ruling path. A list of tasks alone supplies no observable milestone. |
| R2 | Does the rail divide responsibility cleanly? | Identify included work, exclusions, the implementing home and what its neighbour supplies. Trace one cross-boundary case to its actual owner, including who can change the commitment. |
| R3 | How would we know the rail has delivered? | The exit condition points to evidence at the consuming boundary and its authorized observer. Give a case where every contributing unit reports done but the promised result is missing. The exit condition must stay open in that case. |
| U1 | Could the assigned worker start or stop correctly? | The unit names a bounded change, allowed surfaces, inputs, dependencies and the next authorized act. Follow its instructions on the selected checkout and confirm the prerequisite disposition. |
| U2 | Can the intended check catch the relevant mistake? | Run the applicable acceptance path against a known good and a plausible wrong input at identified revisions and settings. Inspect the result and errors. For a human criterion, verify the technical preparation and use a separately attributed human observation for anything only that person can establish. |
| U3 | What happens at interruption or handoff? | Walk a retry, failure or handoff for this unit. A successor knows what already happened, how to resume safely, which person acts if needed, and what evidence returns to the authoritative record. A final human judgment may still be pending. |

“Road” means the ordering and dependencies *between* outcomes. “Rail” means the scope and exit of *one* intended outcome. A specification can live in ruled decisions or existing project documents. A small project can satisfy several questions with one record. The same artifact may support different claims, but repeating it never adds another scored outcome. For a manual task use an observable manual case. Creating a new spec file or automating a preference carries no points in itself.

The rubric adapts requirements traceability and testability, dependency sequencing, and staged verification against observable behavior. The criteria and weights above and below are proposed design choices. The primary engineering references do not supply a validated Charter index. [NASA, requirements and verification matrices](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/) · [GAO, schedule assessment guide](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-16-89g.pdf) · [Google SRE, evaluating a bounded rollout](https://sre.google/workbook/canarying-releases/)

## The score and its denominator

1. Freeze the question first. Name the horizon, all promised outcomes within it, their canonical homes and IDs, source revisions or working-tree digests, effective settings, rubric version, assessor and evidence cutoff. Freeze this list before choosing which records to improve. A sample receives a sample label. If the project inventory itself is unknown, withhold a project total.
2. Use the same twelve cells for every outcome. Give each artifact family one quarter of the weight and its three questions equal shares. For a family with known ratings summing to **s** and **u** unknown cells, its interval is **100 × s / 6** to **100 × (s + 2u) / 6**. The corresponding denominator for a complete outcome is 24. Coverage is known cells divided by applicable cells. Unknown stays in the denominator. An interval is a range of possible rubric scores.
3. For the project, average over unique promised outcomes in the frozen horizon with equal weight. A known outcome with zero inspected cells contributes 0–100 and zero coverage. Show all four family scores, outcomes inspected/total, unresolved critical holds, and date beside the overall interval. If a single headline is needed, use the lower endpoint and label it **demonstrated points out of 100**. Round only for display. A missing predecessor, known false pass, unconfirmed execution target or reserved owner act holds the **affected path**. An independent path can continue.
4. Attribute movement cell by cell. Keep the two snapshots, citation, evidence type, rater and reason. Distinguish a repaired artifact, a changed external fact, new evidence about identical bytes, a regression, and assessor disagreement. Compare changed bytes using the same checks and equivalent inputs on both snapshots. Show unresolved cells and lost evidence. A new outcome, purpose, weighting or criterion starts a new series. Publish the overlap on the old denominator alongside the new baseline.
5. Before attributing a repair, have a second assessor score shuffled before/after/control versions against the frozen cases without seeing the author’s labels. Report agreement by cell and any disagreement affecting a hold. Preserve both judgments and leave unresolved claims unknown. An agent may rehearse a human path. The named person supplies the actual observation.

For example, three known unit scores of 2, 0 and 1 give **50/100** at full coverage for that family. Replacing the zero with an unknown gives **50–83.3** at two-thirds coverage. Deleting a failed result cannot improve demonstrated points. With two equally weighted outcomes, one fully supported and one completely uninspected, the project reads **50–100, 50% coverage**. No audited subset can stand in for the missing outcome.

A project card should open on the largest consequential defect: for example, “The unit's trial check accepts a record with no participant. Fixing the check protects the decision to open self-service.” A row underneath explains the delta and opens the evidence. Prioritize a false pass near a release over collecting easy points on remote work. A companion metric records whether the next worker actually picked the right step, repeated a question, reopened work, or delivered the promised outcome. Use the count, observation window and denominator. A perfect artifact score can coexist with an unobserved delivery. [Outcome review example](../examples/outcome-review.md)

## What the two systems would own

| Home | Proposed responsibility | Smallest observable contract |
|---|---|---|
| Charter | Own the portable rubric version, criteria, outcome IDs, evidence bindings and arithmetic. Start with a worksheet. Later a read-only export could use the project's existing parsers and acceptance fingerprints. | The same pinned inputs produce the same total offline. A legacy project with no dedicated specification can cite equivalent sources. Unavailable evidence survives export as unknown. |
| Tele-Claude Project Road | Resolve repository snapshots and runner evidence, retain comparisons and show where preparation breaks down. Display through the existing rail readiness view. | A changed unit, criterion, dependency or evidence digest invalidates its dependent assessment. A failed source read cannot restore a cached green result. Rerunning the same snapshot produces one assessment. |
| Owner Desk | Route the remaining human choice, action or observation, its recommendation and its outcome. Use existing decision and human-check packets. | A broken technical check goes back to the worker. One ready decision for the authorized person survives duplicate discoveries. The receipt records who answered which revision and the authoritative home records its effect. |
| Programme hub | Read qualified references and show dependencies across projects. Worker homes own execution, evidence and their own authorization. | A child receipt references its project, unit, criterion and revision. A changed revision, unreachable home or missing downstream receipt keeps the programme dependency outstanding. |

The serialized assessment needs a version, project and outcome identity, criterion identity, source revisions, evidence references and digests, observed time and scope, assessment state and rater, and a reason for a hold or unknown. Multiple observations may attach to one claim. Compute rollups from the canonical outcome IDs at query time. Keep private evidence at its home and allow an authorized summary with redacted references. A read-only export is an additive interface. Installing package changes later follows the versioned upgrade procedure. [Existing Road design](../design/project-road-control-center.md) · [Verification before owner work](../design/agent-verification-before-owner.md) · [Install and upgrade](../install.md)

A programme is a graph. If website and service both depend on the same shared decision, count that decision once **if it is itself a promised outcome**. Otherwise show it as a dependency with zero extra scoring weight. If the programme promises an integrated result, rate its own distinct interface or acceptance evidence. Never recursively average a parent summary alongside its children. Two paths pointing to one verified result X and one failed result Y can yield 75 by averaging branch averages. Equal weights for the two distinct promised results give 50. If the relation or inventory cannot be reconciled, show the visible scope, withhold the global rollup and identify the unresolved link. Authority stays local even when several projects share an operator.

## Less owner work as evidence accumulates

Lauren Tan describes a team that made cross-context imports fail deterministically and gave agents ways to exercise the application. The transferable practice is to trace a recurring error to the cheapest layer that can reliably catch it. For Charter, make an unresolved dependency impossible to dispatch in the runner, detect a stale criterion by fingerprint, and replay a wrong result through the acceptance check. Reserve prose for an intent or trade-off that the mechanism cannot decide. Ask the assessor to demonstrate which mistake now fails. Banning a particular API, counting PRs or setting a verification-token quota would give this project no such evidence. [Lauren Tan’s AI Engineer talk](https://ai.engineer/talks/O_zcc6Xl12g-all-code-is-slop-except-mine)

Start with interruptions the system can remove today. Reuse an answer only when the actor, question, scope and revision still match. Collect missing technical evidence automatically. Keep reversible in-scope preparation moving under existing authority. Make one entry per actual decision and bring it to the owner when that person can change what happens next.

A good decision asks one question in the owner’s words. It gives the concrete consequence of each choice, a recommendation and reason, the estimate behind it, the missing evidence and what would change the answer. A request to judge quality presents the exact candidate and the relevant example, summarizes the completed checks and isolates the judgment only that person can make. Offer “needs changes” with one free-text reason and retain it for the next attempt. Show comparable issues together when they share a decision. Give separately authorized people their own turns.

For example, an owner previously ruled that a service may be opened to another operator only after three observed uses. The preparer finds three creation receipts and no retrieved results. The worker can diagnose and repair the counter without asking whether three receipts are good enough. Once the complete examples exist, the person sees the exact scope of the remaining question: **“Do these three examples cover the work you need to hand over?”** The packet shows the three use cases, what the receiver actually got, and the consequence of a missing case. A technically valid record does not answer that preference.

Track three different quantities. The **artifact score** measures the evidence for the twelve claims. **Recommendation confidence** is the filer's estimate for a specific choice and can be compared with later choices. **Predicted satisfaction** would forecast a named person's answer to one exact question about one revision, before that person sees it. Record a verdict and any later rework separately. A pass on an observation such as “the screen appeared” provides no satisfaction label. If a reviewer can see the prediction it may influence the answer. Keep the prediction hidden during calibration, and reuse ordinary review verdicts where the question already asks about satisfaction.

| Stage | What happens without an interruption | How to judge advancement |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare | Workers run checks, repair permitted defects, reconcile repeats, and prepare the remaining human question. | Replays produce the same disposition. A deliberately bad result stays blocked. The same unanswered item cannot generate a second request. |
| Predict in shadow | For one repeatable review class, log the predicted event and probability, evidence, scope and misses before the normal human review. | Count correct predictions and false confident passes by band, class and consequence. Report denominator, missing labels, overrides, later rework and assessment cost. Use held-out later cases before proposing any policy change. |
| Delegate a narrow act | The existing delegate can take an act only when the project policy already permits it and deterministic conditions on revision, scope, evidence and reversibility pass. Otherwise prepare the usual handoff. | The owner may separately authorize an expiring class of low-consequence work after seeing the error rate and cost. Record the sample size and the acceptable upper bound on material misses in that ruling. Preserve an audit sample and a way to revoke it. |
| Revert to preparation | A stale revision, contradicted evidence, material miss, changed class, expired authority or sample too small disables the proposed shortcut. | Log why it reverted, recheck affected outcomes and route genuine owner duties through the existing Desk. |

Set tolerance according to the harm of a wrong action. For illustration, even 30 independent representative audited actions with zero material misses have a one-sided 95% binomial upper bound of about 9.5% on the miss probability. The calculation is `1 − 0.05^(1/30)`. Correlated tasks and selectively answered questions weaken that inference. A high percentage alone cannot justify a new authority. Keep personal taste, contractual scope, payments, protected owner changes and another person's assent at their declared home. The immediate opportunity for more autonomy is agent-owned repair and evidence preparation. ## The first release decision

Run two bounded comparisons before writing a package gate. First use a chain with a known contradiction, a cosmetic rewrite and a repair. Second, use a nested chain with a shared dependency, an inaccessible child and a human decision in sequence. Freeze each cohort and its cases. Rehearse a positive, a plausible wrong result, a stale revision and an interrupted retry. Give an independent assessor the records in shuffled order. Observe the next real handoff when it occurs.

Advance the read-only scoring projection if independent assessors can identify the same consequential defect, the repair changes the expected behavior, the cosmetic edit earns no credit, missing evidence stays visible, and the real successor can name the correct next act without a repeat clarification. Record preparation minutes, owner attention minutes and rework per comparable handoff separately from calendar wait. If the scorer rewards prose, a worker can weaken its own denominator, or maintaining it costs more attention than it saves, revise or retire it. Keep shadow prediction experimental until its own evidence supports a narrowly authorized change.

The portable proposal can be tried from the [paired worksheet calculator](score-charter-pair.py). Its input is a CSV with one row per outcome and criterion and columns `outcome,criterion,before,after,evidence`. Supply all twelve rows for every outcome in the frozen list. Use 0, 1, 2 or ?. Record the two source snapshots and holds alongside the worksheet. The calculator performs arithmetic only. It cannot discover omitted outcomes, judge the evidence or authorize work.

Copy the [blank worksheet](charter-quality-worksheet.csv), replace the example outcome ID and prompts with your own evidence, and repeat the block for each promised outcome. For a two-outcome horizon, run the following command.

```sh
python3 docs/proposals/score-charter-pair.py pair.csv --outcome checkout --outcome receipt
```

Declare the IDs from the frozen inventory separately from the CSV. Missing or duplicate cells are errors. An assessor records an explicit unknown for an uninspected cell. An empty inventory has no score.
